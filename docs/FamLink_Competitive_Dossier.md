## 1. Category map (2026)

The market is **three distinct sub-categories**, not one. Knowing which one a competitor lives in tells you how it competes and whether it actually overlaps with FamLink.

**A. Hardware appliances** (wall/counter touchscreen + app + usually a sub). Thesis = *ambient frequency*: a screen the family passes many times a day. Single-home.
**B. App-only mass-market** (phone/web shared calendars). Thesis = *ubiquity + low friction*. Multi-context, but historically fights for daily engagement.
**C. Co-parenting / cross-household** (often legal-grade). Thesis = *coordination across two homes*, sometimes court-ordered. **This is the category FamLink's "cross-family shared events" reframe wades into.**

| Player | Sub-cat | Form factor | Founded | Scale signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Skylight** | A | Hardware + app + sub | 2014 | ~9.3M users; 99% YoY rev growth; $50M debt facility (2025) |
| **Hearth** | A | Hardware + app + sub | 2020 | ~40K families; ~$14.3M raised |
| **Cozyla** | A | Hardware + app, **no sub** | ~2022 | Smaller; Amazon/comparison presence; TIME "best invention" |
| **Cozi** | B | App (no hardware) | 2005 | 20M+ users; **owned by OurFamilyWizard since 2022**; alienated base with 2024 paywall |
| **TimeTree** | B | App (mobile-first) | 2014 (Tokyo) | **70M users**; free + ~$45/yr premium |
| **Google Calendar** | B | App | — | Default; not family-specific |
| **OurFamilyWizard** | C | App | 2001 | **1M+ users, court-ordered in all 50 states**; $99–150/yr per parent; **owns Cozi** |
| **AppClose / Our Days / Kidtime** | C | App | various | Co-parenting; some free tiers |
| **FamLink** | **A?+B+C** | App (web + mobile), **no hardware** | 2026 (in dev) | Pre-launch |

**[Assessment] Three structural facts that matter most:**
1. **Every funded *appliance* competitor is a hardware company.** FamLink is the only serious app-first play aiming at the *organization* (not just co-parenting) use case. Differentiator (no $300–900 barrier) **and** risk (no ambient surface for daily engagement).
2. **The two categories FamLink straddles are converging under one owner.** OurFamilyWizard (co-parenting leader) bought Cozi (family-org leader) in 2022. The exact intersection FamLink targets — *one app for both intra-family org and cross-household coordination* — is being consolidated by an incumbent. Both a threat and validation of the thesis.
3. **App-only can reach massive scale (TimeTree: 70M).** The "no wall = no engagement" fear is not a law of nature. TimeTree did it with *shared events + in-event chat*, not a screen. That points directly at FamLink's already-planned P3-04 Group Chat as the engagement engine.

## 2. Skylight — the category leader

### What it is
WiFi touchscreen family organizer. Started as the **Skylight Frame** (top-selling digital photo frame on Amazon), then extended the same hardware/retail muscle into the **Skylight Calendar**. Sells the calendar as "All-in-One Organization" replacing the family whiteboard.

### Product line & pricing
- **Hardware:** Calendar 2 and **Cal Max**, in **10″, 15″, and 27″**; counter stand or wall mount.
- **Entry price:** Calendar 2 ~**$299.99** hardware.
- **Subscription — "Calendar Plus":** ~**$79/year**. Realistic first-year cost ~**$379**, then ~$79/yr.
- **Free tier (no sub):** tasks, custom lists, profiles, parental lock, automatic updates, companion app.
- **Paid (Calendar Plus):** **Sidekick AI**, meal planning, star-powered rewards, event import.

### AI ("Sidekick")
The strongest AI in the category right now. Snap a photo of a school flyer / sports schedule / invitation → events extracted automatically; forward emails/PDFs → events added; scan fridge contents → recipe suggestions. 2026 reviews single out Sidekick as the standout feature ("a family's dream AI assistant... it works so well").

### Trust & guarantee
4-month money-back guarantee, 24/7/365 support.

### Company / funding
- **Founded 2014**, launched via Kickstarter ($55K, ~2× goal).
- **Bootstrapped profitably for ~a decade**, then debt-financed for growth: $15M (Aug 2023) → facility grown to **$50M** with SG Credit Partners + Wingspire (Apr 2025). *Debt, not dilutive equity — a sign of a profitable, cash-generative business.*
- Angel/early backers named: B Capital, GV (Google Ventures), SV Angel, **Marc Benioff, David Sacks, Ellen Pao, Eric Schmidt**, Trae Vassallo.
- **~9.3M+ users** across Frame + Calendar; claimed **99% YoY revenue growth**.

### Go-to-market — this is the real moat
- **Omnichannel retail:** Amazon (category best-seller), **Walmart, Target, Costco, Best Buy** — physical shelf presence.
- **Retail media spend:** Walmart Connect, Target Roundel, Best Buy Ads.
- **Affiliate program:** flat **$8/unit**, 45-day attribution window.
- **Positioning:** "extreme ease of use" for non-tech family members (kids, grandparents); heavily sold as a **gift** (new moms, grandparents, "tech junkies").

**[Assessment]** Skylight is not a startup we out-execute on features alone. Their advantage is *distribution + brand + profitability*, built over 12 years off the photo-frame cash engine. They own retail shelves and the Amazon algorithm. Their AI (Sidekick) is also genuinely good, which removes "we have AI, they don't" as a wedge. **Compete on category, not on checklist.**

## 3. Hearth — design-forward challenger

### What it is
A 27″ framed touchscreen positioned as **décor, not a gadget** — "built by moms who have been there." Companion app is explicitly secondary (and gated behind the paid membership).

### Product & pricing
- **Hardware:** ~**$629** (reg. $699), 27″, three frame options, wall mount included; financing ~$63/mo.
- **Subscription — "Family Membership":** $9/mo → ~**$5.76/mo** on 3-yr prepay.
- **Free (no membership):** calendars, calendar syncing, unlimited profiles.
- **Paid:** Hearth Helper (AI), meal planning, lists, routines/rewards/streaks, weather, privacy mode, **and the mobile app itself**.
- **Trial/warranty:** 120-day home trial, 1-yr warranty, 30-day membership trial.

### AI
"Hearth Helper" — AI scheduling companion + meal planning. **[Assessment]** Bolted-on and paywalled, weaker/less-hyped than Skylight's Sidekick.

### Company / funding
- **Founded 2020**, NYC; three female co-founders (CEO Mei Lin Ng, CPO Nathalie Stratton, CGO Susie Harrison).
- Public debut **July 2022** (TechCrunch). **~$14.3M raised**, 3 rounds; Female Founders Fund among backers.
- **~40K families** claimed.

### Go-to-market
- **Mom/parenting influencers** on Instagram/TikTok with discount codes (live "PRIME" promo on site).
- Heavy **review-content + comparison-content** ecosystem ("Cozyla vs Skylight vs Hearth," "Best Hearth Alternatives 2026").
- Design/lifestyle press: TechCrunch, NYT, House Beautiful.
- Founder "built by moms" trust narrative.

**[Assessment]** Hearth is the #2-ish *premium design* play, not the leader. Most expensive hardware in the set ($629). Its wedge vs Skylight is aesthetics; its wedge vs us is the same ambient-wall advantage. Beatable on price and reach; respect its brand/press footprint.

## 4. Cozyla — the no-subscription hardware disruptor

### What it is
A 15.6″–32″ AI touchscreen "family command center" sold as a **one-time purchase, no subscription, nothing paywalled.** Positions itself as more open than Skylight/Hearth (runs apps beyond the calendar; smart-home integration). TIME "best invention" badge.

### Product & pricing
- **Hardware:** 15.6″ **$349.99**, 24″ **$699.99**, 32″ **$899.99**.
- **Subscription:** **none** — "no monthly fees... no premium tier... no features locked behind a paywall."
- **AI:** voice assistant **"Hey Cozy"** (voice → calendar events), meal planning with **5,000+ built-in recipes**, ingredient-based recipe suggestions, chore charts.

**[Assessment]** Cozyla's whole wedge is **subscription fatigue** — undercutting Skylight/Hearth on total cost of ownership by killing the recurring fee. That it's a viable position tells you the recurring-sub model in this category is under pressure. Relevant to FamLink pricing: a competitor is already winning customers by *removing* the subscription. FamLink's affiliate-commerce thesis is the software-side version of the same move (monetize commerce, not a mandatory fee).

## 5. Cozi — the cautionary tale (and a live opportunity)

### What it is
The original app-only family organizer: shared calendar, lists, meal planning, recipes, chores. **20M+ registered users** — the largest installed base in the category. **No hardware.** This is the closest analog to FamLink's *form factor*, which makes both its scale and its mistakes instructive.

### Company / ownership
- **Founded 2005** (Robbie Cape, Jan Miksovsky), Seattle.
- Acquired by **Time Inc. (2014) → Meredith → OurFamilyWizard (May 2022).** Now owned by the co-parenting leader.

### Monetization
- Free tier (ad-supported) + **Cozi Gold ~$39/yr** (one sub covers the whole family).

### Why it's a cautionary tale — the 2024 paywall pivot
Cozi shifted from "free with optional upgrade" to an **aggressive freemium**, and its base revolted. Documented complaints (2025–26):
- **30-day calendar cap on free** — can't see or add events beyond 30 days unless you pay. Triggered a wave of churn ("Best Cozi Alternatives 2026: after Cozi Cut the Free Tier").
- **Ads** (including video) in the free tier.
- **Dated UI** — "looks and feels like it was built in 2015," no meaningful refresh in years.
- **One-way sync only** — changes in Google Calendar don't flow back to Cozi. A fragmented, half-broken integration.
- **Privacy** — ad-supported model resells/share data with third parties.
- **Reliability** — freezing, failed loads, repeated forced logins.

**[Assessment] This is FamLink's single most actionable near-term wedge.** There is a large, *actively churning* pool of 20M-scale users who want a modern, app-first family organizer and are searching for alternatives right now. The named gaps — dated UI, one-way sync, ads, weak privacy, no AI — are precisely where an AI-first, two-way-synced, privacy-respecting, commerce-monetized (not ad-monetized) product wins. The window is open *now* and is time-sensitive.

## 6. App-only & co-parenting landscape (the categories we actually overlap)

### TimeTree — proof that app-only scales
- **70M users**, founded 2014 (Tokyo), mobile-first shared calendar; dominant in Japan.
- Free (ad-supported) + **Premium ~$4.49/mo / $44.99/yr** (ad-free, attachments, event priority).
- Engagement engine = **shared calendars + chat *inside* events** + memos/to-dos. **No hardware, no wall — and still 70M users.**
- **[Assessment]** The existence proof that app-only family coordination reaches mass scale. The mechanism is *event-centric collaboration + communication*, which is exactly FamLink's planned shape (events + P3-04 group chat). Modest ARPU, though — app-only monetization is thin unless you add a second revenue line (FamLink's affiliate thesis).

### OurFamilyWizard (OFW) — the co-parenting incumbent that now owns Cozi
*The strategic gorilla over FamLink's exact intersection. Profiled in depth because of that.*

**Company / ownership**
- **Founded 2001** by Larry Patterson (with Jai Kissoon) — literally **invented the shared-parenting-app category.**
- **2020: growth investment from Spectrum Equity** (a serious tech-focused PE firm); brought in pro CEO **Nick VanWagner**. This is what funded the move into growth mode — and the **2022 acquisition of Cozi** (20M users) to extend beyond divorce into all-family coordination.
- **1M+ users, 75 countries.**

**Position & pricing**
- **Recommended / court-ordered in all 50 US states** — the app judges name directly in custody orders. This is the moat: distribution by *judicial mandate*, not marketing.
- **$99–150/yr per parent** (both parents pay separately — effectively $200–300/yr per family). Financial-need discounts exist.

**Features** — built for *high-conflict, documentation-mandated* situations:
- **ToneMeter** (AI sentiment analysis flagging hostile message tone), **documented/immutable timestamped messaging** (admissible as evidence), **Info Bank** (shared family records), expense splitting, shared calendar. **No GPS/location** (that's AppClose et al.).

**Weaknesses — and they are severe:**
- **User ratings are abysmal:** ~1.3–2.1 stars across Trustpilot / SiteJabber / Google (100+ reviews each). This is not a beloved product.
- Complaints: "$100+/yr for a 3rd-rate text/email service"; outdated, cumbersome UI; crashes, lags, and **failed message notifications** (some serious enough to involve police/lawyers); calendar/journal/expense modules called "woeful, isolated, cumbersome" with constant double-handling; billing problems (charged after cancellation, refund difficulty); ToneMeter's effectiveness doubted; a "false sense of hope" that the app enforces cooperation.

**[Assessment]** The gorilla is **hated by its own users and protected almost entirely by court mandate, not product quality.** That's the key insight:
- **Don't attack the court-ordered segment** — it's captive (low churn regardless of satisfaction) and FamLink would have to become a legal-evidence product to compete, which contradicts the warm-family brand.
- **Do attack the *voluntary, low-conflict* segment** — amicable co-parents, blended families, and grandparents who are NOT under a court order and have no reason to tolerate a 1.5-star, $200–300/yr adversarial tool. OFW serves them badly because its entire UX assumes conflict and litigation. That is real, exploitable space, and it's exactly where FamLink's cross-family reframe points.
- The Spectrum Equity + Cozi combination means a well-capitalized player is consciously building the intra-family ⨯ cross-household product FamLink envisions. **Validation of the thesis and the most direct strategic threat, simultaneously.**

### Second-tier co-parenting apps
- **AppClose** (free, popular), **Our Days**, **Kidtime** (genuinely free tier), **ClanPlan**, **FamilyWall**. Borrowable mechanics: per-relationship **"circles"** (separate calendar + messaging per arrangement), **location/place alerts** ("Alex arrived at school"), and **fair task assignment** with completion tracking to defuse disputes.

### ⚠️ The AI-family-organizer startup wave (2025–26) — the lane is NOT empty

**This is the most important finding in the dossier and it directly contradicts a core FamLink assumption.** The "app-first, AI-first family organizer" space — the exact lane FamLink is betting on — is in a **2025–26 funding/launch gold rush** with multiple well-built, well-funded entrants. The watch-list assumption "any app-first entrant (the lane we're betting on being empty)" is **already false.**

| App | Founded | Backing / team | Model | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ohai.ai** | ~2023 | **$44.45M raised**; founder **Sheila Lirio Marcelo (Care.com founder)**; NEA, Eniac, Muse Capital; angels Olivia Munn, Mindy Kaling, Abby Wambach | AI household assistant | The heavyweight. "Reduce the mental load." Serious money + celebrity-grade credibility. |
| **Nori** | 2025 | **ByteDance + Samsung veterans** (Domus Next); launched Jan 2026 | "Free to organize, pay for power" (AI usage-priced) | **150K+ active families by Mar 2026** — explosive growth. Multimodal input, **conflict detection**, emergency assistant. |
| **Calendara** | 2024 | — | Free / ~$3–5/mo | AI photo→event **90%+ accuracy**, **two-way Google sync standard**, modern UX. Built to eat Cozi. |
| **Maple** | — | — | Free + generous tier, no device | Cozi-switcher positioning. |
| **Jam** | — | "two moms" | Shared hub | Forward-email→event. |
| **FamilyWall** | established | — | Free + ~$45/yr premium | Calendar + location + messaging. |

**[Assessment — this reframes the whole strategy]**
- **FamLink's foundational bet — "we're the app-first AI-first family app in an open lane" — is largely invalidated as of 2026.** Ohai ($44M, Care.com pedigree) and Nori (ByteDance vets, 150K families in ~2 months) are doing precisely this, funded and fast. "App-first + AI + family" is now a *category*, not a wedge.
- **The table-stakes have moved up sharply.** Multimodal capture (voice/photo/email→event), two-way sync, conflict detection, AI meal planning — all already shipped by free or near-free competitors. FamLink does not currently have these and would be *behind* on them, not ahead.
- **What survives as genuine FamLink differentiation** (because none of these have it): the **cross-family graph** (extended/blended/cross-household — they're all single-family), the **sports vertical depth**, and **commerce-based monetization** (lets FamLink be cheaper than usage-priced-AI rivals). These are no longer "nice extras" — **they are the entire defensible thesis.** Everything generic about FamLink is now contested by funded teams.
- **Honest implication:** FamLink cannot win as "a better general AI family organizer." It can only win as "the family app for the relationships and activities the others structurally ignore." Narrow and deepen, or get out-executed by better-funded generalists.

## 8. FamLink SWOT (against this field)

**Strengths**
- **No hardware barrier.** $0 entry vs $300–629. Acquire a family with a download.
- **AI-first by default**, not a paywalled add-on — *if* the coordination AI is genuinely better than Sidekick (a high bar now).
- **Cross-family graph / shared events.** Blended/divorced/extended families and teams — things a single-home wall screen *structurally cannot do*.
- **Vertical depth** (sports-as-family-feature) where incumbents stay deliberately horizontal.
- **Affiliate-commerce monetization** with no inventory/COGS/returns logistics.

**Weaknesses**
- **No ambient surface.** The wall screen is a feature, not just a cost — it solves the daily-engagement problem we still have to solve in software. (Maps to our documented "attack frequency" risk.)
- **No brand / trust / installed base.** Skylight has 9.3M users and Benioff/Schmidt money; Hearth has NYT/"built by moms."
- **Not in the conversation.** Competitors own the comparison-content + retail-shelf real estate. We have zero share of search.
- **Skylight's AI is already excellent**, neutralizing a naive "we're the AI one" pitch.

**Opportunities**
- Serve the **much larger TAM that won't buy a $300–629 screen.**
- Win **multi-household** use cases the appliances can't address.
- Out-coordinate (not out-display) via propose-confirm AI on high-frequency events.
- Affiliate commerce as a cleaner, more scalable revenue engine.

**Threats**
- **Skylight/Hearth ship free companion apps.** If they make the app great and free, our "no hardware" edge narrows while they keep the wall as upsell.
- **Skylight's distribution + profitability** can simply outspend and out-shelf a startup.
- **App-only family tools historically struggle to stay daily-active** (see Cozi). This is the existential risk; the wall players have already solved it with glass.
- **The app-first AI lane is now a funded gold rush** (Ohai $44M, Nori, Calendara, Maple, Jam). FamLink is *late and currently behind on table-stakes AI features* (multimodal capture, two-way sync, conflict detection). Generic "AI family organizer" positioning is no longer available — see §6 AI-wave.

## 9. Ideas & areas to think about

These are **prompts for discussion, not decisions.** Labeled by type. Nothing here is authorized or locked.

### A. Wedges (near-term, offensive)
1. **The "Cozi refugee" play — real demand, but a CONTESTED red ocean (revised down from "highest-conviction").** Cozi's May 2024 change (30-day free calendar cap, ads, dated UI, one-way sync, data resale) sent "millions of families" searching for alternatives — the demand is real and well-documented. **But** the deeper dig found the space is *already crowded* with AI-first startups explicitly chasing these refugees: **Nori** (AI assistant, voice/photo/email capture; free core + AI pay-as-you-go), **Calendara** (AI photo→event extraction), **Wimely** (meal-planning-centric), **FamilyWall** (calendar+location+messaging), plus TimeTree and Google Calendar DIY. An entire "Best Cozi Alternatives 2026" content economy now exists.
   - **[Assessment — correction to last turn]** I previously called this the single highest-conviction wedge. That was too strong. The refugee demand is genuine, but "be a modern AI family calendar" is *exactly* what Nori/Calendara/Wimely already are, and some are ahead on the specific battleground feature (AI photo/email→event capture). The 2026 narrative shift is explicit: from "enter everything yourself" → "connect your email and let AI detect it." **If FamLink enters this race on calendar-AI parity alone, it's late to a crowded field.**
   - **What still makes it winnable for FamLink specifically:** the things the refugee-chasers *don't* have — the **cross-family graph**, the **sports vertical**, and **commerce monetization** (so it can undercut on price). The play isn't "another Cozi alternative"; it's "the family app that also does the cross-household + activity-coordination things Cozi never could." Capture refugees as a *beachhead*, not the whole thesis.
   - **Prerequisite to even compete:** AI photo/email→event capture and true two-way sync are now table stakes, not differentiators. Must-have to be in the conversation at all.
2. **Two-way calendar sync as a headline feature.** Cozi's one-way sync is a named, repeated pain point; even Skylight's value is partly "syncs everything into one view." If FamLink nails *true bidirectional* Google/Apple/Outlook sync, that alone out-features the incumbent app. Table-stakes that the leader fails at = free differentiation.
3. **Privacy as positioning, not just policy.** Cozi resells data; newer entrants (OurCal) win on "end-to-end encryption, no ads, no data resale." This aligns with the existing product-direction thesis ("protect the family-graph + trust franchise"). The family graph is the most sensitive data we hold — making *we don't sell your family's data* an explicit brand promise is both honest and a competitive wedge against ad-supported incumbents.

### B. The engagement problem (the existential one)
4. **TimeTree (70M, app-only) proves the wall isn't required — but the mechanic is.** Their daily engagement comes from *shared events + chat inside the event*. FamLink's planned **P3-04 Group Chat** is the same lever — it may be more strategically central than its backlog position implies. **Worth thinking about:** is in-event conversation a P3-04 "feature," or is it actually FamLink's core retention engine?
5. **"Bring your own screen" / kiosk mode — neutralize the wall cheaply.** The appliances' one true edge is the always-on glance surface. FamLink could offer a **free display/dashboard mode** that runs full-screen on any old tablet, spare phone, or cast to a TV — giving families the ambient surface *without* selling $300–900 hardware. This converts the competitors' biggest moat into a $0 software feature. **[Assessment]** Potentially the highest-leverage idea here: it directly answers "but they have a wall and we don't."
6. **Notification discipline.** Parents complain about family alerts getting lost in the notification flood. A well-designed **daily/weekly digest** ("here's today" / "here's the week") + meaningful badges could be a retention surface app-only tools underuse.

### C. Cross-family / co-parenting (the convergence zone)
7. **Own *amicable* cross-family; cede the courtroom.** OFW owns high-conflict/court-ordered (immutable logs, ToneMeter, evidence). That's a compliance product with adversarial UX. The under-served space is **low-conflict** blended families, grandparents, and amicable co-parents who find OFW's conflict-assuming tone alienating and overpriced ($99–150/yr *per parent*). FamLink's cross-family-shared-events reframe is well-aimed *if* it stays in the warm/amicable lane and doesn't try to be evidence-grade.
8. **Borrowable mechanics from co-parenting apps:** per-relationship "circles" (separate calendar+chat per arrangement), place/arrival alerts ("Emma left soccer"), and *fair* task assignment with completion tracking. These map naturally onto a family-graph model and the sports vertical (pickup/dropoff coordination is a real, frequent pain).

### D. Monetization & pricing
9. **Subscription fatigue is real and exploitable.** Cozyla wins hardware customers by *removing* the sub; Cozi's base revolted when it *added* paywalls. FamLink's affiliate-commerce thesis is the structural escape: it could let FamLink be **free (or near-free) on subscription and monetize commerce**, undercutting every sub-dependent competitor. The risk to watch: ad/affiliate monetization must not become the data-resale behavior we'd be criticizing Cozi for. Keep affiliate ≠ surveillance.
10. **The gift-market blind spot.** Skylight's biggest acquisition channel is *gifting* (grandparents, new moms) — a box you can wrap. App-only products can't be gift-wrapped, which is a real structural disadvantage in this category. **Worth thinking about:** is there a giftable SKU (gift subscription, "set up Grandma's view," a physical gift card / starter kit) so FamLink isn't locked out of the channel that drives a big share of category sales?

### E. Distribution (the hardest gap)
11. **Win the comparison-content battlefield.** Buyers in this category decide via "X vs Y vs Z" articles and YouTube reviews. FamLink is absent from all of it. Getting *into* that content (as the app-first alternative) is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.
12. **Reverse-engineer the affiliate playbook.** Skylight pays flat $8/unit, 45-day window; Hearth uses influencer discount codes. FamLink's own affiliate thesis can borrow these mechanics for *user* acquisition (mom/parenting/sports-parent influencers), not just commerce monetization.

### F. AI (raise the floor before claiming the ceiling)
13. **Match the table stakes first.** Sidekick's photo-of-flyer→events, forward-email→events, and meal planning are now *expected*. FamLink's propose-confirm AI must at minimum ingest a photo/email/PDF into events, or "AI-first" rings hollow next to Skylight.
14. **Then differentiate on coordination, not extraction.** Everyone can extract an event. Few can *coordinate* one across a family graph — detect conflicts across households, propose who drives, reconcile two parents' calendars, chase RSVPs. That's where propose-confirm + the graph could genuinely lead.

### G. Naming note (observation, not a recommendation)
Steve's read — "none of them have catchy/descriptive names except OFW" — is **half right, and the nuance is useful.** The category splits cleanly into two naming conventions:
- **Warm/evocative metaphors:** Hearth, Skylight, Cozi, Cozyla, Nest-adjacent. These are actually *strong* brand names — Hearth (fireplace/home) and Skylight (light into the home) are genuinely good. So "none are catchy" is too harsh; the hardware leaders chose evocative names deliberately.
- **Plainly descriptive/functional:** OurFamilyWizard, FamilyWall, TimeTree, Wimely, Nori, Calendara. Clear about what they do; less emotionally warm.

**Where this lands for "FamLink":** it sits in the *descriptive/functional* bucket (Fam + Link) — same family as OurFamilyWizard and FamilyWall. Two honest takeaways:
1. **Reassuring:** descriptive names clearly work in this market — OFW is the category-defining leader with a clunky name. A name does not have to be poetic to win. Function-forward is a legitimate, proven choice.
2. **Caveat:** OFW wins on **court mandate, not brand love** (1.5-star ratings, users mock the name). So "the leader has a plain name" isn't proof a plain name *drives* success here — it succeeded *despite* the name, via a moat FamLink won't have. If FamLink's positioning is the *warm, amicable* family product (vs OFW's cold legal tool), a name with more warmth could reinforce that contrast. If the positioning is utility/platform ("Pro Organizer" B2B, the family graph), "FamLink" is perfectly serviceable.
- **[Assessment]** Not worth agonizing over pre-launch. "FamLink" is clear and safe. The real question is positioning (warm-family vs utility-platform); pick that first, and whether the name needs more warmth answers itself. Don't let naming block product progress.

- **Skylight Calendar app roadmap** — does the free app get good enough to use *without* the hardware? (Erodes our core edge.)
- **OurFamilyWizard + Cozi integration** — do they merge intra-family + cross-household into one product? That's a direct shot at FamLink's intersection.

The lane is *not* under-occupied — track it as the primary competitive front.
